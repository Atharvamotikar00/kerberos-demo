What is Kerberos?

Kerberos is a network authentication protocol built around one core idea: prove who you are without ever sending your password across the network — not even once, not even encrypted. Instead, you prove you know your password by using a key derived from it to encrypt a timestamp, which only someone holding the matching key could have produced correctly.

It also gives you single sign-on: authenticate once with your password, and use the resulting ticket to access any number of services afterward — without typing your password again — and mutual authentication: not only does the service verify you, you get cryptographic proof that the service is the real one, not an impostor.

Client

You. Knows only your password — never any server's secret key.

AS ?

Authentication Server. Verifies your password-derived key, issues a Ticket Granting Ticket.

TGS ?

Ticket Granting Server. Trades your TGT for short-lived tickets to specific services.

App Server

The service you actually want to use. Only trusts tickets it can decrypt with its own key.

AS and TGS together are usually called the KDC ? — the Key Distribution Center. You're about to walk through all six message hops below, with real ciphertext at every step.

The six message hops

This is the reference diagram for the exchange, matching RFC 4120's structure. It's reused below with the active hop highlighted as you progress.

  • AS-REQ / AS-REP — client authenticates, receives a Ticket Granting Ticket.
  • TGS-REQ / TGS-REP — client trades the TGT for a ticket to a specific service.
  • AP-REQ / AP-REP — client presents the service ticket; server proves itself back (mutual auth).
